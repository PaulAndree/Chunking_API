# Summary

The mechanism of mutagenicity of chloroethylene oxide (CEO), an ultimate carcinogenic metabolite of vinyl chloride, was investigated in 3 Escherichia coli strains (E. coli "multitest"). In this system, the mutagenicity of CEO was found to be mainly SOS-independent. CEO did not induce recombinational events at a detection level of about $1 0 ^ { - 2 }$ recombinants/survivor. Our results indicate that CEO- (or vinyl chloride-) induced bacterial mutagenesis arises mainly from miscoding DNA adducts.

Chloroethylene oxide (CEO), a potent mutagen in bacteria [1-3], yeasts [4] and mammalian cells [5], can induce tumours in rodents [6]. It generates base-pair substitution but not frameshift mutations [2,3]. We recently investigated the mutational specificity of CEO in $E$ coli trpA mutants [7], whereby $\mathbf { G C } \to \mathbf { A T }$ transitions and, to a lesser extent, $\mathbf { A T }  \mathbf { T A }$ transversions were induced. In order to investigate whether this specificity arises through one (or several) expected miscoding DNA adduct(s), we have used the $E ,$ coli "multitest" of Toman et al. [8]. In this system, 3 different effects of DNA-damaging agents can be characterized: mutagenesis, SOS induction and homologous recombination. SOS- (or recA-) dependent mutagenesis results from an error-prone replication activity, the induction of which is triggered by the presence of non-coding DNA alterations which subsequently can be miscopied in the course of DNA synthesis. Mutagens, like radiations, which act through this pathway are called "indirect" mutagens [9]. "Direct" mutagens, like methylating and ethylating agents, act independently of the SOS system; their mutagenicity arises from direct miscopying of miscoding DNA adducts [9].

Our data show that CEO mutagenesis in $E$ coli and possibly in other organisms (see Introduction) occurs mainly through the "direct" pathway, i.e. may result from the persistence of one or more miscoding DNA adducts. Studies on trpA reversion in $E .$ coli have shown that $\mathbf { G C }  \mathbf { A T }$ transitions are favoured over other base changes [7]; attempts to identify miscoding DNA adducts responsible for the mutational specificity of CEO are in progress [1115].
