# Phenomenological Theory of Repair Kinetics of Ultraviolet Irradiated Lambda Phage\*

S. L. FORNILI, L. CORDONE, AND M. RADMAN1

Istituto di Fisica dell'Università and Gruppi Nazionali Struttura della Materia of the Consiglio Nazionale delle Ricerche, Palermo, Italy; and Centre des Faibles Radioactivités, C.N.R.S., 91, Gif-sur-Yvette, France

Accepted October 27, 1970

A theoretical derivation of survival curves of UV-irradiated λ phage, in different phage-bacterium systems (lacking or possessing excision and/or recombination repair) is presented. The mathematical expressions are satisfactory fitted with experimental points, and the validity and self-consistency of the model is shown.

# INTRODUCTION

It has been suggested (Radman et al., 1970) that recombination and excision are complementary mechanisms in UV radiation damage repair of DNA, excision repair being effective in the absence of DNA replication, and recombination repair being effective after and/or during replication. [For a review on repair mechanisms, see, e.g., Howard-Flanders (1968).]

Within the frame of the proposed model, it has also been suggested (Radman et al., 1970) that above a certain critical dose, DNÁ replication is inhibited for a sufficiently long time for excision repair to take place. This inhibition would be promoted by radiation damage in the DNA region(s) that control DNA replication. The critical dose should therefore be the one capable of giving (on the average) each irradiated particle one hit that inhibits DNA replication. It follows that recombination repair will be efficient for low UV doses and excision repair for high UV doses.

In the present paper we start from the above model to derive theoretical expressions for the survival curves of UV irradiated phage λ in various phage-bacterium systems. The fitting with experimental points is very satisfactory and self-consistent. The different steps in repair are visualized by a block-diagram representation, and the mathematical tools are very simple. The fitting was performed with a leastsquares iterative computer program, using data obtained in several independent experiments.

# MATERIALS AND METHODS

Materials and methods for experimental determination of survival curves have been reported in a previous paper (Radman et al., 1970).

We recall that:

a. Escherichia coli rec- mutants are defective in genetic recombination (Clark and Margulies, 1965; Howard-Flanders and Theriot, 1966; van de Putte et al., 1966).

b. Escherichia coli uvr" mutants are unable to excise (Boyce and Howard-Flanders, 1964; Setlow and Carrier, 1964) pyrimidine dimers induced by UV irradiation (Beukers and Berends, 1960; Wacker et al., 1960).

c. Phage λ red- mutants are defective in autonomous vegetative genetic recombination (Signer and Weil, 1968).

Phage suspension less than 1 mm thick were irradiated at a concentration of about $\mathbf { 1 0 ^ { 8 } }$ particles/ml in Tris-Mg-gelatin medium.

Phage assays were carried out by standards techniques as described by Adams (1959). Phage were preadsorbed and photoreactivation during experiments was avoided. For phage assays, exponentially growing bacteria have been used in all the experiments.

# Systems Lacking Excision and Recombination Repair (red-/rec-uvr-)

Experimental results (see below) show that the survival curve for irradiated phage λ red- assayed on rec-uvr- bacteria is an exponential function, of the type:

$$
S ( D ) = S _ { 0 } e ^ { - a D }
$$

where $S _ { 0 }$ is the initial number of phages; $\boldsymbol { s }$ is the number of phages surviving at dose $D$ , and $\pmb { a }$ is a parameter measuring the probability for the phage DNA to receive a hit that is lethal in this system. Eq. (1) follows from the rate equation:

$$
\frac { \mathrm { d } S } { \mathrm { d } D } = - a S
$$

We may visualize Eq. (2) in terms of a box O (see Fig. 1), which contains $\mathcal { S } _ { 0 }$ phages at the beginning of UV irradiation, and in which the number of phages vs. UV dose decreases at the rate given by Eq. (2).

Survival at dose $D$ will be given by the number of phages still remaining in box O.

# CONCLUSIONS

The agreement between experimental and theoretical curves definitely shows that (a) excision repair alone is effective above the critical dose, i.e., when damage to DNA is such that the beginning of DNA synthesis is delayed; (b) conversely, the recombination repair alone is effective below this critical dose; (c) as expected, when both repair systems are present, a cross term (visualized by box ER in Fig. 4) must be introduced.

We shall not attempt here a thorough physical interpretation of the quantitative relationships among the different parameters obtained in the present work. An additional and interesting piece of information, however, can be obtained through a comparison of the $g$ and $^ { a }$ values; since $g \approx \% a$ , the target size of the DNA region(s) controlling the replication of $\lambda$ DNA is about $\%$ that of the whole $\lambda$ DNA. The fact that $g \approx \%$ $\pmb { a }$ also indicates that the lethality in red-/ rec-uur- system need not be ascribed to damages in the replication controlling region(s). An alternative interpretation, consistent with the present results, could be that in red-/rec-uvr" systems with very low UV dose DNA replication may start but the damage prevents its completion. The same interpretation is also consistent with recent results (Horii and Suzuki, 1970) showing that in irradiated Escherichia coli rec-uvr- double mutant only a small degradation of DNA takes place close to the replicating point.
