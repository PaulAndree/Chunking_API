# Protection and Host Cell Repair of Irradiated Lambda Phage II. Irradiation of 5-Bromouracil-Substituted Phage with Near Visible Light

M. RADMAN \*, A. ROLLER\*\* and M. ERRERA Laboratoire de Biophysique et Radiobiologie, Faculté des Sciences, Université libre de Bruxelles

Received March 10, 1969

Summary. Phage A, in which thymine has been totally substituted by the analog, 5-bromouracil, have been irradiated with near visible light in the presence and absence of cysteamine, a free radical scavenger and proton donor. These phage were then assayed on normal bacteria and on mutants which are unable to repair DNA lesions. It is concluded that these phage suffer three different kinds of lesions: (1) a class which are not reparable, but which can prevented cysteamine, suggesting that they are sugar damages; (2) a class which are reparable but are not eliminated by cysteamine and (3) a class which can be neither eliminated nor repaired. The nature of the latter two types of lesions cannot be surmised at present.

# Introduction

Theincreased sensitivity toultraviolet (UV) and near visible (NV) light, of phage in which the thymine is replaced by the analog 5-bromouracil (BU), was first demonstrated by STAHL e tal. (1961). Photoreactivation and host cell reactivation (HCR) have not been observed in UV irradiated BU-phage (STAHL et al., 1961; SAUERBIER, 1961). It has been suggested the BU substitution inhibits these repair reactions (STAHL et al., 1961; SAUERBIER, 1961; HoWARD-FLANDERS et al., 1962).

The repair of BU-A has been examined, following irradiation with NV light, whicht inactivates BU-substituted, but not normal phage (STAHL et al., 196l). The effect of cysteamine (Cy) has als been examined. It is known to protect BU phage exposed to UV irradiation (HoTz, 1963). As Cy ist the product of hydrolysis of A.E.T. (2-aminoethyl isothiouranium bromide) at neutral pH, it is probably also responsible for the protection from NV light observed with A. E. T. (Fox and MEsELson, 1963). Cy is thought to act as a proton donor and scavenger of free radicals (BACQ and ALEXANDER, 1961; ORMEROD and ALEXANDER, 1963).

The results of the present experiments demonstrate that NV irradiated BU-X suffer three classes of damage. One class is neither repaired by bacterial repair systems nor prevented by the presence of Cy. Another is also irreparable, but it is prevented by Cy. The third is reparable, andnot affected by Cy. The possible nature of some of these classes of lesions is discussed, in the light of the results of recent investigations of the nature of BU photochemical lesions.

# Discussion and Conclusions

The major photoproduct of NV light irradiated BU-DNA is known to be uracil (WACKER, MENNIGMANN and SzYBALSKI, 1962). Some of the arguments indicating that uracil formation is harmless are discussed by HoTz and ReuschL (1967). When irradiation takes place in the presence of Cy, a Cy-uracil addition product may be formed, as suggested by the finding that, in the case of UV irradiation, such a product is formed by cysteine (SmrTH, 1964). If this in fact happens, then it is most likely that the Cy-uracil addition product is also harmless, as there does not seem to be any important lethal effect of Cy.

When NV light debrominates BU-DNA, presumably free radicals are formed, as has been demonstrated in the case of UV (HuTcHinson and HALEs, 1967). These have been shown to be capable of causing alterations in the deoxyribose moiety which are associated with chain breaks (KoEHNLEIN and HuTCHINson, 1966; HuTCHINSoN and HALEs, 1967; HoTZ and REuSCHL, 1967). Such breaks do not rupture a sugar-phosphate bond (HuTcHinsoN and HALEs, 1967) and are therefore not a substrate for polynucleotide ligase (WEISs and RIcHARDsoN, 1967). With respect to the uvr $C$ repair system, if these "sugar breaks" cause no deformation of the DNA, they would not be recognized by excision enzymes (review HowARD-FLANDERs, 1968). Cy has been shown, in the case of UV irradiation, to prevent such breaks. It therefore seems reasonable to suppose that the damages which, in the present experiments, are not repaired by the excision repair system which are prevented by Cy, are, in fact, "sugar breaks". Using electron microscopy, this conclusion has been corroborated by the finding of fragmented DNA obtained from BU-A, NV light irradiated in the absence of Cy (unpublished results). The size of the fragments suggests that the breaks occur in regions which, according to denaturation maps (InMAn, 1967 and this laboratory, in preparation) are particularly rich in adenine and thymine.

A second class of lesions is susceptible to excision repair, but is not affected by Cy. Presumably these lesions involve sterical deformations, but their nature is unknown. Many fewer such lesions are repaired by the bacterial recombination system. It should be noted that the results obtained with rec- bacteria are difficult to analyze, because the phage contains complementary recombination genes (red, and perhaps int) (EcHOLs and GINGERY, 1968; SIGNER and WEIL, 1968). We have been unable to prepare BU labelled int- red- λ.

BU-λ are slowly inactivated by NV light, even in the presence of Cy and all bacterial repair systems. The nature of these lesions is also unknown.

A clear conclusion can be drawn from these experiments, namely, that BU gives rise to some lesions which are susceptible to repair by the bacterial DNA repair systems, and thus, these systems are not inhibited by BU substitution.
