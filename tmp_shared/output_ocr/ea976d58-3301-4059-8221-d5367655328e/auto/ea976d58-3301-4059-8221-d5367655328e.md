# SUMMARY

The efficiency of excision repair of UV-damaged $E$ . coli DNA is enhanced when DNA replication is temporarily blocked. This block, in the presence and absence of excision repair, was obtained in a strain of $E$ . coli, thermosensitive for DNA replication and in its excision-deficient derivative. It is suggested that the regulation of DNA synthesis plays an important role in excision repair.

# INTRODUCTION

A model for the mechanism of excision repair of pyrimidine dimers produced in DNA by UV-light has been proposed,7,15. It explains fairly satisfactorily the recovery of biological activity of repaired DNA and the efficient elimination of potentially lethal and mutagenic lesions.

In E. coli, excision repair of UV-damaged bacterial and phage DNA is controlled by the uvr genes8,17 and it probably occurs through the following steps (for review see ref. 6; illustration in Fig. 3A): $( \tau )$ recognition of the UV-induced lesion; (2) incision of the damaged strand by an endonuclease; (3) excision of the lesion and degradation of neighbouring nucleotides; $( 4 )$ repair replication of the degraded segment; and (5) covalent joining by polynucleotide ligase. If this model is correct, excision repair can only be efficient in non-replicating segments of DNA because repair replication requires the intact complementary DNA segment as template (Fig. 3). Normal, semiconservative DNA replication during excision repair would lead to discontinuous daughter double helices, i.e., to lethality (see DiscussIon).

In a previous paper12, we demonstrated in the case of $\lambda$ phage that the inhibition of replication of UV-damaged DNA favours excision repair. In the present work, similar results are obtained for uninfected $\varepsilon$ .coli. An $E$ . coli strain with DNA ts and its excision-deficient derivative (uvr $\mathbf { B } ^ { - }$ were used in order to control DNA replication, both in the presence and absence of excision repair. Excision repair during the temporary blockage of DNA replication is enhanced; this supports the currently accepted model for excision repair of DNA.

# DISCUSSION

All post-irradiation conditions causing an inhibition of DNA replication, without also inhibiting excision repair, increase the survival of bacteria capable of excision repair $\left( u v r ^ { + } \right)$ . Metabolic conditions causing the inhibition of DNA synthesis such as liquid holding4, amino acid starvation and irradiation during the stationary phase of growth5,6, increase the survival of $\scriptstyle { u v r ^ { + } }$ but not of uvr- bacteria. These effects become more evident if the bacteria are deficient in genetic recombination. The survival increase, which occurs when DNA synthesis is inhibited, does not occur in the presence of inhibitors of excision enzymes5. It is therefore reasonable to suppose that the increased resistance is due to the prolonged action of excision repair when DNA replication is postponed.

These results clearly show that excision repair of damaged $E$ . coli DNA is highly efficient when DNA does not replicate before or during repair. If DNA replication takes place before excision of the dimers, these can be bypassed during replication13 and gaps are expected to appear in the newly synthesized strand, opposite each dimer13 (see Fig. 3B). Obviously, the excision of such dimers in replicated DNA will cause double-strand breaks (illustrated in Fig. 3B) which cannot be repaired by known mechanisms. In addition, excision opposite a gap may not occur”, perhaps for steric reasons, and the lesion will not be repaired by excision.

On the other hand, if replication passes regions of DNA where excision repair has not been completed (see Fig. 3A), the replication of excised template strands will probably give only pieces of DNA. The proof that DNA can be more completely repaired by excision if the replication is blocked is a further argument supporting the current interpretation of the excision repair mechanism. Similar results have already been presented for UV-irradiated $\lambda$ phage12.

DNA replication and repair. We discussed in a previous paper12 the probable importance of the control of DNA replication for the efficiency of DNA repair. We suggest as a working hypothesis that there is a switch from excision to recombination repair in $\gamma e c ^ { + } \ u v r ^ { + }$ bacteria which is correlated with DNA replication12. In the strains of bacteria used, experiments of type I (see Table I), show that the control of DNA replication does not seem to be optimal for the most efficient repair by bothrec and uur functions. Otherwise, no survival increase of $G Y \ g _ { 5 3 } \ ( \mathrm { D N A _ { t s } \ } u v \gamma ^ { + } )$ bacteria would be expected after a thermal treatment and subsequent overnight incubation at $3 2 ^ { \circ }$ , without any inhibitor. The important survival increase, due to enhanced excision repair during the block of DNA replication, suggests that at $3 2 ^ { \circ }$ , DNA replication starts before the optimal switch from excision to recombination repair.

Increased UV-resistance of wild-type bacteria after a liquid holding, starvation or when irradiated during the stationary phase of growth also indicates that in physiological conditions the switch from excision to recombination repair (i.e., DNA replication) occurs before DNA repair is optimal.

Inactivation at $4 \pi ^ { \circ }$ of $U V$ -irradiated uvr- bacteria. It is difficult to understand the cause of inactivation at $4 \boldsymbol { \mathsf { I } } ^ { \circ }$ of bacteria $G Y \ g _ { 5 2 }$ (DNA ts, uvr B), GY I113 $\mathrm { \Omega } ^ { \mathrm { ( D N A ^ { + } } }$ , uvrBand $G Y I I I 4 \left( \mathbf { D N A ^ { + } } , u v r ^ { + } \right)$ , which have been previously irradiated. A somewhat clearer situation appeared when we used a $\lambda \mathrm { ~ O ~ } _ { \mathfrak { t s } }$ mutant12, which does not replicate at $4 2 ^ { \circ }$ . UV-irradiated λ Ots was strongly inactivated upon exposure to $4 2 ^ { \circ }$ in $\gamma _ { \mathrm { { c } } \mathrm { { c } } ^ { + } }$ uvr A but not in rec A uvr A host. Therefore, we suggested that the rec-controlled nucleases may inactivate the non-replicating, UV-damaged DNA.

In conclusion, an important increase in viability of UV-damaged $E$ .coli $G Y _ { 9 5 3 }$ $\left( \mathrm { D N A } _ { \mathrm { 1 s } } , \ u v r ^ { + } \right)$ caused by a temporary block of DNA replication at $4 ^ { \tau ^ { \circ } }$ , seems to be due to the prolonged action of excision repair, when DNA does not replicate. This effect is inhibited by PF, absent in a normally replicating $( \mathrm { D N A ^ { + } } )$ derivative and in an excision-deficient derivative.
