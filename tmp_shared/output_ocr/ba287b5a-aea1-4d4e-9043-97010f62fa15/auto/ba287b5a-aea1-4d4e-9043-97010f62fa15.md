# Protection and Host Cell Repair of Irradiated Lambda Phage III. Ultraviolet Irradiation of 5-Bromouracil-Substituted Phage

M. RADMAN\*, A. ROLLER\*\* and M. ERRERA Laboratoire de Biophysique et Radiobiologie, Faculté des Sciences, Université libre de Bruxelles

Received March 10, 1969

Summary. 5-bromouracil-containing A phage, irradiated with UV light in the presence and absence of cysteamine, were assayed on normal and DNA-repair deficient bacteria.

It has been found that these phage suffer (1) lesions, of unknown nature, which are neither reparable by the bacterial DNA repair systems nor prevented by cysteamine, a free radical scavenger and proton donor; (2) lesions which are converted to reparable lesions by cysteamine, and which may be sugar damages (perhaps accompanied by pyridimine dimers) which are converted to simple dimers; and (3) irreparable lesions, which do not occur in the presence of cysteamine, and which are probably sugardamages. Theselatter are the most important, and in large part explain the increased sensitivity to irradiation of 5-bromouracil-substituted phage.

# Introduction

Ultraviolet (UV) iradiation of phage in which the thymine has been replaced by 5-bromouracil (BU) gives rise to lesions which cannot be repaired by DNA repair systems (STAHL et al., 1961; SAUERBIER, 1961; HoWARD-FLANDERS et al., 1962). This was interpreted as meaning that the repair enzymes are inhibited by BU substitution. Recently, it has been found that the repair enzymes are not inhibited in partially BU-substituted, UV -irradiated bacteria (L1oN, 1968). A significant protection of BU substituted T 1 and T 4 is observed if the phage are irradiated in the presence of cysteamine (Cy) (HoTz, 1963; HoTz and REusCHL, 1967), who suggested that it permits normal DNA repair.

In none of the above investigations was use made of mutants in which the repair systems are nonfunctional. A study of the UV inactivation of BU-substituted phage A has therefore been undertaken, with the aim of distinguishing the effect of repair system from changes in the lesions resulting from the presence of Cy.

Phage, irradiated in the presence or absence of $\mathrm { C y }$ , were assayed on normal, host cell reactivation (uvr $C ^ { - }$ and recombination $( r e c A ^ { - } )$ deficient bacteria. It has been found that the most important effect ist the partial prevention of lethal lesions by Cy. In agreement with the investigations cited above, there is little or repair when phage are UV irradiated in the absence of Cy. In its presence, a significant fraction of lesions are reparable. These findings are interpreted in the light of recent chemical studies of the nature of UV induced lesions. They are also compared to results obtained with BU $\lambda$ , irradiated with near visible light, and with normal, UV irradiated λ (RADMAN et. al., 1969b, a).

# Discussion and Conclusions

The results demonstrate that the fraction of reparable lesions which occur when BU-X are irradiated with UV, either in the presence or absence of Cy, is small, although it is larger in the former case. Reparable lesions are probably pyrimidine dimers (review, HowARD-FLANDERs, 1968), whose number might be increased by the presence of Cy. Such an increase could be due either to an increase in the absolute number of such lesions, as opposed to nonreparable lesions, or to the elimination of irreparable sugar damages which may otherwise accompany dimers (HoTz and ReuscHL, 1967). If sugar damages are present, the dimer could in principle be excised, but the lesions could nevertheless not be "healed" because they lead to breaks which are not a substrate for ligase (WeIss and RicHARDson, 1967).

Cy changes the nature of half to two thirds of the lesions, rendering them non lethal or non-existent. It is known that, in the case of UV irradiation of BU-DNA "sugar breaks" (HoTz and REusCHL, 1967), strand breaks (LIoN, 1966) and even chromosome breaks (TRosKo and BREwEN, 1967) are suppressed by Cy. "Sugar breaks", which are a consequence of debromination and accompanying free radical formation (HuTcHinson, 1967), may lead to uncontrolled degradation of the DNA (AoKI et al., 1966; BoYcE, 1966). Sugar breaks, in the absence of Cy, are accompanied by transformation of the BU moiety into uracil (WAcKER, 1961). Presumably, uracil formation, which also occurs in the presence of Cy, is a nonlethal lesion when it is not accompanied by a sugar break.

A comparison with the previous paper shows that the results of UV and near visible (NV) irradiation of BU λ are similar. In both cases, the major photochemical reaction is debromination, leading, in the absence of Cy, to sugar damages which give rise to strand breaks other than sugar-phosphate breaks. However, in the case of NV irradiation, in the presence or absence of Cy, there are many more reparable lesions, the nature of which cannot as yet be surmised.

A comparison of UV irradiation of BU and normal λ indicates that the increased sensitivity of the former is due to the nature and quantity of released free radicals. Radical damages, which are protected by Cy, are reparable in the case of thymine-containing λ, but are not reparable in the case of BU-containing λ. In the latter case, they are also more numerous. In thymine DNA, the damages are probably (reparable) pyrimidine dimers, whereas in BU-DNA, they include (nonreparable) sugar breaks. This may well be the major cause of the sensitization to radiation which results from substitution of thymine by BU.
