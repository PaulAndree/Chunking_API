The observation that antipain inhibits TPA-induced SCEs but not MNNG or MNU induced SCEs can be reconciled by assuming that the molecular events leading to SOEs are rate limited by two factors, recombinational activity and substrate susceptibility to these enzymes, That is, SCEs can be induced either, by increasing the level of the recombinational enzyme activity or by providing more DNA sites susceptible to these enzymes by introducing DNA gaps and breaks, TPA itself does not damage DNA, therefore any SCE inducing effect is likely to be due to the activation of recombination enzymes, Conversely, ONA-damaging agents probably increase SCEs by activating the substrate rendering it susceptible to constitutive DNA recombinational repair enzymes. The formation of non-sister chromatid exchanges would therefore appear to require at least one rate-limiting process not involved in MNNG or MNU induced SOE formation. Since the process is antipain inhibited, we postulate that it is inducible32.

Finally, to summarise the possible role of chromosomal changes in multi-step theories ofcancer. TA is not the ony promoter and it is highly unlikely that mitotic recombination is the only mechanism of promotion, It seems more probable that promotion by enhancing the frequency o all chromosomal rearrangements leads to aberrant mitotic segregation events which render certain cells homozygous for the original recessive chromosomal change permitting expression of the malignant phenotype. Bloom's syndrome (in addition to elevated S0e levels), Fanconi's anaemia and ataxia telangiectasia, three human cancer prone diseases, are all characterised by high spontaneous levels of chromosomal instability. It is interesting to speculate in view of the observations made with antipain that chromosome aberrations represent a rate-limiting step in carcinogenesis, probably promotional, mutagenesis alone rarely, if ever achieving complete carcinogenesis. At this point it is interesting to speculate that the mechanisms of initiation and promotion are not mutually exclusive. Chromosomal aberrations could be the basis for both, carcinogenic initiation e.g. deletion mutagenesis, and promotion (chromosomal rearrangements to homozygosity). For example, the qD-deletion of chromosome 13 in cells from retinoblastoma patients may well represent an inherited initiation event requiring a second segregational or mutational event for expression of the transformed phenotype.
