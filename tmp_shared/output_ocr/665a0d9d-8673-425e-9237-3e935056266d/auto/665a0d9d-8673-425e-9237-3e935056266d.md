# SUMMARY

The various mechanisms for maintaining the fidelity of DNA base sequences during replication and repair are described. In excision-deficient bacteria an attractive possibility is that Uv-induced mutations may arise as a result of the inhibition of the 3' to 5' exonuclease proof reading function of DNA polymerase IIl, either by means of the induction of a specific inhibitor or by means of feed-back inhibition by nucleoside 5' monophosphates. There is at present no convincing evidence for this model and little evidence that the process has any significant effect on the viability of Uv-irradiated bacteria.

In bacteria wild-type for repair functions, a model for UV-mutagenesis is proposed which postulates that after low to moderate Uv doses, mutations reflect the normal infidelity of DNA polymerases (mainly III) acting during the filling of excision gaps. Such infidelity is manifested because the normal strand-directed mismatch correction system is postulated to be largely inoperative at regions away from the normal semi-conservative replication fork. This type of mutagenesis would be "untargeted", i.e. the mutations are not necessarily exactly at the site of a UV photoproduct, and show a linear dose response curve. At higher doses excision gaps may uncover photoproducts on the complementary DNA strand which may result in "targeted" mutagenesis and be revealed as a quadratic component to the dose response curve.

# SUMMARY

This is a review of our largely unpublished experiments which demonstrate the existence of an efficient DNA base pair mismatch repair system which acts selectively on the newly synthesized strands and hence corrects replicational errors. The chemistry of discrimination between the old and new strands is based upon adenine methylation of GATC sequences controlled by the E. colf dam gene: newly synthesized DNA strands are transitorily undermethylated.

We have isolated and characterized E. coli mismatch correction deficient mutator mutants and have obtained experimental support for the postulated involvement of mismatch correction in intragenic recombination. The mutation spectra of strand discrimination deficient dam mutator mutants and of mismatch correction deficient mutator mutants (mut H, L, S and U) indicate that this mismatch repair acts preferentially upon transition and frameshift mismatches.

# INTRODUCTION

This paper is a chronological review of largely unpublished experiments started in 1976 in the Harvard Biological Laboratories when M.R. was a visiting scientist. Repair of mismatched base pairs in DNA was first postulated to account for gene conversion in eukaryotes. Mismatch repair was also invoked to account for the difference between high and low efficiency markers in bacterial transformation². The role of mismatch correction in recombination. Current theories of the generalised genetic recombination implicate formation of relatively long hybrid DNA segments in which each parental molecule contributes one complementary strand. Such hybrid DNA, which can be formed with and without associated crossing over of flanking parental DNAs, is the region in which gene conversion and high negative interference phenomena are thought to occur as the consequence of repair of mismatched base pairs which are created when one or more genetic markers fall in the hybrid (heteroduplex) region of the recombined DNA. High negative interference (HNI) is the excess of recombinants among closely linked genetic markers ha beensudi  da a $x ^ { 2 1 } , 2 2$ matris thought that HNI is due to creation of recombinant genotypes by the repair of mismatched bases in the hybrid DNA rather than by true crossovers between the two close markers 14,22 This predicts that HNI should be abolished in mismatch correction deficient mutants. Indeed, we have found that in a four factor cross between λ Pam3 and Pam80 close intragenic markers, and cI and Rts2 as flanking well-separated markers, only Pam recombinants were suppressed by a factor of ten in mutS and mutL mutants, whereas cI-R recombinants were unaffected in these replication deficient crosses This provides evidence for the involvement of mismatch repair in intragenic allelic recombination.
