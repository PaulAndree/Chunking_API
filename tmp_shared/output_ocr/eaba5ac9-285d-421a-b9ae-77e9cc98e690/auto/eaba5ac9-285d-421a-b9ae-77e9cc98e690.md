# CELLULAR PARABIOSIS & ORGAN SPECIFICITY OF METASTASES: TESTING A HYPOTHESIS

Miro (27.07.2019)

# Premises:

The aim of this project is to test the hypothesis that cellular parabiosis (i.e., the trans-cellular functional complementation) determines tissue selectivity of metastases (M. Radman, Open Biol. 2019).

This hypothesis posits that the latency period for carcinomas in solid tissues (average about 20 yr, e.g., after atomic bomb explosions) is due to phenotypic suppression of malignant cells by healthy neighboring cells. Each potential metastatic cell homing among healthy cells of the same organ has to pass the same kind of 20 yr latency as the original cell of the primary tumour. Tumour suppressing functions provided by cells of the primary organ encountered in other organs will also suppress metastatic growth of invading cells; only organs unable to provide phenotypic suppression allow can home metastases. This might be the reason for the rarity of metastases in the organ of the primary tumour. Parabiotic suppression of malignancy requires the structures (e.g., TnTs and GJ) and specific contents (e.g., expression of specific tumour suppressors (TS) lost in primary tumour). Hence, the tissue selectivity of metastases.

In addition to this tumor-suppressing c-parabiosis, there is also a tumorstimulating c-parabiosis (e.g., the case of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAF) in the stroma) based on phenotypic suppression of diverse metabolic deficiencies in tumor cells. These metabolic defects are selectively purified by selective cell death (anoikis) of tumor cells while travelling through the bloodstream. Thus, only when tissues provide for specific tumor suppressive functions lost in tumors will c-parabiosis suppress metastatic growth; otherwise c-parabiosis may increase the metabolic fitness of emerging metastases (e.g., the effect of CAFs).

The prediction that organs expressing many TS will rarely home diverse metastases (and those expressing few TS, e.g., the liver, will be frequent home for metastases) seems to be substantiated (Tinka’s work). Now, we want to use DNA and RNA sequencing to identify TS functions lost in primary tumour and provided by tissues resilient to metastases.

IS “FEEDER LAYER” TECHNIQUE A CASE OF C-PARABIOSIS?

(14) Feeder layer - usually fibroblasts irradiated with low dose of X-rays - is a classical technique to grow some “difficult” cells, e.g., embryonic stem cells. Direct contact is required for the effect. Low X-ray irradiation is among the TnT-stimulating stresses. Hence, the provision by the feeder

layer of factors missing in standard media (for particular cells) could be a case of c-parabiosis (like CAFs for tumours).
